The Pacific Coast and the Salmon People

A short excerpt from the Smithsonian’s Pacific Northwest lesson. It leaves out the photo captions, the interview quotes, and the links to the other two case studies. The original page is mostly maps and images, so it is worth a visit.

Original source Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian, Native Knowledge 360° — “Pacific Coast Case Study, Pacific Northwest History and Cultures” (opens in a new tab)

The Native People of the Pacific Coast have plenty of sea life. It helps support their economies and diets. Halibut and shellfish are the mainstay of many coastal nations. But one fish matters most to their shared identities, their cultures, and their relationships with other nations. That fish is salmon.

The Salmon People

sacrificenoun

the giving up of something valuable

Native Nations of the Pacific Northwest call themselves Salmon People. They see salmon as an extremely important gift of food from the Creator. Each year they honor the salmon’s sacrifice in special ceremonies. Many geographic regions set Native Nations and language groups apart in the Pacific Northwest. The original page shows three major regions. They are the Pacific Coast, Puget Sound, and the Columbia River/Plateau. The regions are far apart, and the cultures are diverse. Still, salmon brings together Native People and Nations across the Pacific Northwest.
