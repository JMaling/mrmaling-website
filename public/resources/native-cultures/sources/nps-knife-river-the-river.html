The Upper Missouri River Valley

For hundreds of years, the Upper Missouri River Valley was a lifeline. The river wound through a harsh land. It drew Northern Plains Indians to its wooded banks and rich soil. The earthlodge peoples hunted bison and other game, as the nomadic tribes did. But they were mostly farmers. They lived in villages along the Missouri River and its tributaries. The Knife River is one of these tributaries. It rises more than eighty miles to the west. The Hidatsa made their home where it flows into the Missouri.

The Hidatsa and the Rivers

The Hidatsa developed a way of life that was perfectly suited to the Knife and Missouri Rivers. They built earthlodges from cottonwood and river willow that grew along the banks. They made pottery from river clay. They bathed in the cool water. They also traveled long distances on the rivers, which were like natural highways. Spring floods made the land fertile again. This led to large harvests in the fall.
