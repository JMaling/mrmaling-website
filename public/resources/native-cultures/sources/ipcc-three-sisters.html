The Three Sisters: Corn, Beans, and Squash

How corn, beans, and squash work together as crops and as food. This excerpt is the “Monthly Feature” section of a blog post from the Indian Pueblo Cultural Center. It leaves out the rest of the post, which covers days of observance, a seed library, teaching resources, and books.

Corn, beans, and squash are the Three Sisters. Many Indigenous communities grow types of this trio. This is true from the Northeast to the Southeast, from the Plains to the Southwest, and into Middle America. The name “Three Sisters” comes from the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois). Different communities have different stories. The common thread is that the three sisters are very close. They are stronger together than apart.

The three are one form of companion planting. Companion planting is a farming method where two or more crops are planted together in a single plot. The three work together as a unit to give a higher crop yield. They help each other grow. They create rich soil that resists damage from diseases and insects that would normally eat and destroy them.

companion plantingnoun

planting two or more crops together in one plot

yieldnoun

the amount of a crop that is grown

First, plant the corn. The stalks provide a pole for the beans to wrap around. They also help hold the corn steady in wind. Beans provide nitrogen to fertilize the soil. The large, spiny squash leaves give shade, help the soil hold moisture, prevent weed growth, and discourage insects from invading. Each of the three attracts helpful insects that prey on the ones that cause damage.

fertilizeverb

make soil richer so plants grow better

When eaten together, corn, beans, and squash make a complete and balanced meal. Corn is low in protein but provides carbohydrates. Beans are a rich source of protein and have amino acids that corn lacks. Squash provides different vitamins and minerals than corn or beans. All three can be dried and used for food all year.

carbohydratesnoun

nutrients in foods like corn and bread that give the body energy
