GRAPES Resource Library

West

The West includes many different environments and peoples. These sources focus especially on Chumash communities of coastal California and Native communities of the Columbia River and Pacific Northwest.

Start with the overview

Read the Khan Academy article first. Use it to identify big regional patterns before you investigate specific communities.

Open Khan Academy
Research reminder: Do not write “people in the West all…” unless you have evidence from multiple communities. Chumash life on the California coast was not the same as life along the Columbia River.

G — Geography

The relationship between people and the natural world.

Guiding question: How did the natural world shape where people lived and how they lived?

Columbia River Inter-Tribal Fish Commission: Indians & Tribes

Explore rivers, salmon, seasonal movement, foods, and Native nations of the Columbia Basin.

Smithsonian NK360: Pacific Coast

Use maps and images to connect coastal environments with food, settlement, and travel.

R — Religion

The shared ideas, values, and practices that guide how people think about life, the world, and their place in it.

Guiding question: What shared beliefs, values, and practices helped people understand life and the world?

CRITFC: Tribal Salmon Culture

See how salmon connects spirituality, ceremony, identity, and daily life.

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Myths, Games & Dances

Explore oral traditions, ceremonial dances, animals, and sacred beliefs.

A — Achievement

The inventions and ideas that people develop to solve problems and improve their lives.

Guiding question: What inventions or ideas helped people solve problems or improve their lives?

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Daily Life

Study tomols, basketry, fishhooks, food processing, homes, and other technologies.

NPS: Chumash Tomol

Learn how plank-built ocean canoes were designed and constructed.

P — Politics

How people make decisions as a group.

Guiding question: How did people make decisions, choose leaders, or organize their community?

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Life

Look for evidence about towns, villages, leadership, and community organization.

NPS: Native Inhabitants of the Channel Islands

Use the pre-contact sections to investigate chiefs, specialists, and settlements. Stop before the section on missionization.

E — Economics

The study of how we make, trade, and use goods and services.

Guiding question: How did people make, get, trade, and use goods and services?

Oregon History Project: Columbia River Trade Network

Trace salmon, shells, obsidian, hides, baskets, and other goods through a major trade center.

NPS: Navigation, Trade & the Tomol

Use the section on trade to study shell-bead money and island-mainland exchange.

S — Social Structure

How people are divided into groups and roles.

Guiding question: What groups and roles existed, and how were responsibilities divided?

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Life

Investigate houses, villages, family life, specialists, and ceremonial spaces.

Smithsonian NK360: Pacific Northwest History & Cultures

Compare several Northwest nations instead of treating the region as one society.

Project focus: Native cultures before European arrival. Record the specific people or community described by each source.
