Early U.S. History Native Cultures Presentation

West

The West includes many different environments and peoples. These sources focus especially on Chumash communities of coastal California and Native communities of the Columbia River and Pacific Northwest.

Start with the overview

Read the Khan Academy article first. Use it to identify big regional patterns before you investigate specific communities.

Read the Overview
Research reminder: Do not write “people in the West all…” unless you have evidence from multiple communities. Chumash life on the California coast was not the same as life along the Columbia River.

CRITFC: Columbia River Tribes — Land, Food, and Homes

Explore rivers, salmon, seasonal movement, foods, and Native nations of the Columbia Basin.

Smithsonian NK360: Pacific Coast

Connect the sea, shellfish, and salmon to Native nations of the Pacific Coast. Use the original link for its maps and images.

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Life

See where Chumash villages were built (near lagoons, creek mouths, lakes, and springs) and how the coast and islands shaped life.

NPS: Native Peoples and Settlement of the Channel Islands

See where Chumash villages were, which islands had fresh water, and how people used both the ocean and the coastal mountains.

R — Religion

CRITFC: Tribal Salmon Culture

See how salmon connects spirituality, ceremony, identity, and daily life.

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Myths & Dances

Explore oral traditions, a sky story, ceremonial dances, animals, and sacred beliefs.

A — Achievement

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Daily Life

Study tomols, basketry, fishhooks, food processing, homes, and other technologies.

NPS: Chumash Tomol

Learn how plank-built ocean canoes were designed and constructed.

P — Politics

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Life

Look for evidence about independent towns and villages, and how communities were organized.

NPS: Native Peoples and Settlement of the Channel Islands

Investigate chiefs, social classes, and settlements.

NPS: Xaxas, a Chumash Village

Look for evidence of a powerful chief, high-ranking families, and tomol owners in one village.

E — Economics

Oregon History Project: Columbia River Trade Network

Trace salmon, shells, obsidian, hides, baskets, and other goods through a major trade center.

NPS: Navigation, Trade & the Tomol

Shell-bead money and island-mainland exchange.

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Daily Life

See how bead money was made and how its value was measured (see “Bead Money”).

S — Social Structure

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Life

Investigate houses, villages, sweathouses, and ceremonial spaces.

Santa Barbara Museum: Chumash Daily Life

Look for signs of status, such as a chief’s larger house and bearskin cape (see “Shelter” and “Dress”).

NPS: The Tlingit of Sitka

Compare a Northwest Coast society: clan gatherings, the potlatch, and how a host raised his social rank.
